How to build a quest

Turn any unit or task into an escape room or treasure hunt — in about five minutes, with help from an AI you already use.

Build a quest Quest library

There is no app to install, no login, and no cost. You write a short description, an AI writes the puzzles, and you share a link. That is the whole idea.

The three steps

  1. 1

    Describe

    Tell the builder your unit or task — for example, “Macbeth, Act 1”.

  2. 2

    Paste into your AI

    Copy the ready-made prompt into ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude. Paste its reply back.

  3. 3

    Play & share

    Check it looks right, then send the link to your class.

Step 1 · Open the builder

Click Build a quest (top of this page). You will see a short, numbered form: Step 1 of 4.

Step 2 · Describe your quest

  • In the big box, type your unit or topic. You can also paste a poem, an extract, or your task sheet.
  • Choose Escape room (solve locks in order) or Treasure hunt (collect letters to spell a word).
  • Pick the year group, the number of stations (5 is a good start), and the difficulty.
  • Leave “Let the AI choose” on if you are not sure which puzzles to use.

Only the description is required. Everything else already has a sensible setting. Click Next.

Step 3 · Get your prompt

The builder writes a prompt for you. Click the big Copy the prompt button. It says Copied ✓ when it is done.

Step 4 · Paste it into your AI

  • Open your school AI — ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude. (There are quick links on the builder page.)
  • Paste the prompt and press enter.
  • Wait a few seconds. Then copy the whole reply the AI gives you — even if it has extra words around it.

Step 5 · Bring the reply back

Back in the builder, click I have the reply, paste it into the box, and click Build my quest. You will see one of three results:

  • 🟢 Green — all good. Go to the preview.
  • 🟡 Amber — built, with a few tidy-ups. The app lists what it cleaned up. This is normal.
  • 🔴 Red — it could not read the reply. Click Copy a fix-up prompt, paste that back to the AI, and try again. You are never stuck.

Step 6 · Check and tidy

The preview shows every station. Click a station to read it the way a student will. To change a clue or an answer, just click the text and edit it. If one station looks broken, click Remove station.

Step 7 · Share with your class

  • Click 🔗 Share link and then Copy share link.
  • Paste that link into Google Classroom, an email, or onto the board.
  • Students click the link and play straight away — on a laptop, tablet, or phone. No login needed.
  • Prefer a file? Click ⬇ Export to download the quest, and share that instead.

What is the AI actually doing?

The AI only writes the words of the puzzles. The quest game, the answer-checking, the hints, and the certificate are all built into Quest Forge. The app also checks the AI’s reply and cleans it up, so a small mistake will not break your quest.

Tips for great quests

  • Keep the topic focused — one text or one part of a unit works best.
  • Paste your own extract or task sheet so the puzzles use your material.
  • Five to six stations is plenty for one lesson.
  • Play it yourself once before you share it.
  • Add a few alternative answers in the preview (for example, British and American spellings).

If something goes wrong

  • “The AI added extra words.” That is fine — the app removes them automatically.
  • “The reply would not build.” Use Copy a fix-up prompt and paste it back to the AI.
  • “The share link is very long.” Your quest is big — share the downloaded file instead.
  • “A correct answer was marked wrong.” Open the station in the preview and add that answer to Accepted answers.
