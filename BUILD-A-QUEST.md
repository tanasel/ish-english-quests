# How to Build a Quest

A simple guide for English teachers.

You do **not** need to know any code. You just describe your lesson, copy one block of text into an AI you already use, and paste the reply back. The app does the rest.

It takes about **5 minutes**.

---

## What you are making

A **quest** is a fun game made of English puzzles. Your class plays it on a laptop, tablet, or phone.

There are two kinds:

- **Escape room** — students solve puzzles **in order** to "escape." One after another.
- **Treasure hunt** — students solve puzzles in **any order**. Each one gives them a letter. The letters spell a secret word.

You choose which kind you want.

---

## Before you start

You need:

1. The **Quest Forge** website open in your browser.
2. An **AI helper** you already use. Any of these is fine: **ChatGPT**, **Gemini**, or **Claude**.

That's it. No passwords. No setup.

---

## Step 1 — Open the builder

1. Go to the Quest Forge home page.
2. Click the button that says **Build a quest**.

You are now in the builder.

---

## Step 2 — Describe your lesson

This is the only part where you type.

Fill in the boxes. Use short, plain words.

- **Topic** — what the lesson is about. For example: *Of Mice and Men — loneliness and friendship*.
- **Source text** (optional) — if you want puzzles from a poem or passage, paste it in.
- **Year level** — for example: *Year 8*. This tells the AI how hard to make it.
- **Mode** — pick **Escape room** or **Treasure hunt** (two big tiles).
- **Number of puzzles** — pick a number from 3 to 8. Start with **5**.
- **Difficulty** — pick **Gentle**, **Standard**, or **Challenge**.
- **Puzzle types** — leave **"Let the AI choose"** turned on. It will pick a good mix.
- **Theme** — pick a fun flavour, like castle, pirate, detective, or library.

When you have written your topic, the **Next** button turns on.

Click **Next**.

> **Good to know:** You only have to fill in the topic. Everything else has a sensible setting already.

---

## Step 3 — Copy the prompt

The app now writes a **prompt** for you. A prompt is just a clear set of instructions for the AI.

You do **not** need to read it or change it.

1. Click the big button **Copy the prompt**.
2. You will see **Copied ✓**.

That's the prompt saved to your clipboard, ready to paste.

---

## Step 4 — Paste it into your AI

1. Open your AI helper in a **new tab**. (The app has handy links to ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude — they just open the tab. Nothing is sent automatically.)
2. Click in the chat box.
3. **Paste** the prompt. (Press **Ctrl + V** on Windows, or **Cmd + V** on a Mac.)
4. Press **Enter** to send it.

The AI will think for a moment, then write back a long block of text. That block is your quest.

---

## Step 5 — Copy the AI's reply

1. **Select all** of the AI's reply.
2. **Copy** it. (Press **Ctrl + C**, or **Cmd + C** on a Mac.)

Don't worry if the reply looks messy or has extra words around it. The app will tidy it up.

---

## Step 6 — Paste the reply back

1. Go back to the Quest Forge tab.
2. Find the big box that says **Paste the reply**.
3. **Paste** your reply into the box.
4. Click **Build my quest**.

Now watch the colour of the result:

- 🟢 **Green** — perfect. Your quest is ready. Go straight to preview.
- 🟡 **Amber** — the app fixed a few small things and lists them. Read the friendly notes, then click **Looks good — preview**.
- 🔴 **Red** — something wasn't right. You are **never stuck**. You can click **Try auto-fix again**, or **Copy a fix-up prompt** to send back to the AI, or edit by hand.

Most of the time it is green or amber.

---

## Step 7 — Check the preview and fix any flags

You can now see your quest the way students will.

- On the **left** is the list of puzzles. A **green tick** means a puzzle is fine. An **amber dot** means one needs a quick look.
- On the **right** you can click any puzzle to see it and **edit it**.

To fix a flagged puzzle:

1. Click the puzzle with the amber dot.
2. Click the part you want to change. Type your fix.
3. Click away (or press **Tab**) to save it.

If a puzzle can't work at all, the app shows a clear banner with **Edit** or **Remove**. Removing one is fine — the others renumber neatly.

When everything looks good, you are ready to share.

---

## Step 8 — Share the quest with your students

At the bottom you will see these buttons:

- **▶ Play (test)** — try the quest yourself first. Always a good idea.
- **🔗 Share link** — get a link to send to students.
- **⬇ Export** — download the quest as a file.
- **💾 Save to my quests** — keep it for next time.

To share with a class:

1. Click **🔗 Share link**.
2. Copy the link from the box.
3. Open **Google Classroom**.
4. Make an **announcement** or an **assignment**.
5. **Paste the link** and post it.

Students just click the link. The quest opens straight away.

---

## What students see when they play

When a student opens the link:

1. They read a short **story introduction**.
2. They see the puzzles.
   - In an **escape room**, they solve them **one at a time**. Each solved puzzle unlocks the next.
   - In a **treasure hunt**, they pick **any puzzle**. Each solved puzzle gives them a **letter**. The letters appear in a little tray. When they have them all, they spell the **treasure word** to win.
3. If they get stuck, they can press **Hint**. There are three hints. The first is a gentle nudge. The last almost gives the answer.
4. When they finish, they see a **well done** screen and can **print a certificate** with their name on it.

Students play **on their own device**. Nothing they type is saved or sent anywhere. There are **no timers** and no rush.

They can also turn on the **dyslexia font**, **bigger text**, **dark mode**, or **high contrast** from the top of the screen — just like you can.

---

## Tips for great quests

- **Start small.** Five puzzles is a great first quest.
- **Give a clear topic.** "Macbeth — ambition and guilt" works better than just "Macbeth."
- **Paste your own text** when you can. Puzzles built from your class poem or passage feel more relevant.
- **Always play it yourself first** with the **▶ Play (test)** button. You'll spot anything odd in two minutes.
- **Match the level.** If a puzzle feels too hard, build a new one set to **Gentle**.
- **Save your good ones.** Use **💾 Save to my quests** so you can reuse and tweak them next term.

---

## If something goes wrong

**"The AI added extra words around the quest."**
That's fine. The app **cleans it up automatically**. Just paste the whole reply and click **Build my quest**.

**"The result came up red."**
You are not stuck. Click **Copy a fix-up prompt**, paste that into your AI, and it will send back a cleaner version. Paste that back in.

**"The share link is very, very long."**
This happens with big quests. Instead of the link, click **⬇ Export** to **download the file**, and share that file with your students. They can open it from the player's **Open a quest** button.

**"A student says their right answer was marked wrong."**
Open the quest in the builder, find that puzzle, and add the student's wording to the **Accepted answers** list. The app accepts every wording you list there. Then re-share.

**"The link won't open for a student."**
Ask them to copy the **whole** link. Long links sometimes get cut off in a message. Sharing the downloaded **file** always works.

**"I want to change a puzzle after I shared it."**
Edit it in the preview, then make a **new** share link or file. Send the new one to your class.

---

## In short

1. **Describe** your lesson.
2. **Copy** the prompt into your AI.
3. **Paste** the reply back.
4. **Preview** and fix anything flagged.
5. **Share** the link in Google Classroom.

That's a whole English quest, ready for your class. Have fun with it.
